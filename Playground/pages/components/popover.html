Popover Component

A non-modal dialog anchored to a trigger — interactive content that no ancestor can clip.

Basic Usage

The trigger is the element immediately before the popover. The panel is a real dialog: it carries role="dialog" and the trigger gets aria-haspopup="dialog" plus a managed aria-expanded.

The panel below contains a nui-select, which opens its own popup. That combination is the interesting case: the platform light-dismisses an open popover when a click lands outside it, and "outside" is judged by DOM ancestry — so an inner dropdown that is a DOM descendant of the panel is nested and leaves the panel open.

Explicit Target

When the DOM structure prevents adjacency, point the popover at its trigger with for. The element carrying the popover must be a real <button> — the component wires it as the platform's own invoker rather than with a click listener.

Placement

placement accepts a side or auto, which picks whichever side has room. Every result is clamped into the viewport, so a panel that cannot fit degrades to the nearest visible position instead of rendering off-screen.

Programmatic Control & Events

The component exposes show(), hide(), toggle() and isOpen(). Both events fire for every path in and out — invoker click, light dismiss, Escape, or a programmatic call — because they are emitted from the platform's own toggle event.

Wide Panels Centre Horizontally

A popover may be up to 80vw wide, so it can hold a real form rather than a couple of rows. Past 60% of the content frame's width, the bubble body centres horizontally across the frame rather than being pushed off-screen with the trigger, but remains firmly anchored vertically to its button with the pointer arrow aimed directly at the control.

Popover, Tooltip or Dialog?

Pick by interaction, not by looks. nui-tooltip is non-interactive help on hover — it must not contain focusable content. nui-dialog is modal and screen-placed: it makes the page behind it inert and cannot anchor. nui-popover is the anchored, interactive, non-blocking one.

Note that the panel needs an accessible name. Give it aria-label or aria-labelledby; a nameless role="dialog" is announced as "dialog" and nothing else, and the component logs a warning.
